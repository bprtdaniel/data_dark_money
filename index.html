Which Elections Take Dark-Money?

A visual look at where undisclosed political spending appeared in competitive 2024 House races.

“Donald Trump will work to lower your taxes, vote Trump.”

“Donald Trump will work to lower your taxes, that’s great!”

What is the difference between those sentences?

According to U.S. campaign finance rules, the first is a direct endorsement of a candidate, making it political campaigning.

The second, however, can be framed as issue advocacy or “social welfare” activity rather than direct candidate support.

Under U.S. tax law, a 501(c)(4) organization is a nonprofit organization operated for social welfare purposes.

These groups can spend money in politics, but they are not required to disclose donors in the same way traditional campaign committees or SuperPACs are.

They are often referred to as "Dark Money" Organizations.
